Back in [the renovation plans](/posts/intro-to-house-renovation/), "Add solar panels and battery storage" sat at the bottom of the list. It has moved up.

## Why now

The list met the energy crisis. Russia [invaded Ukraine on 24 February 2022](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-europe-60504334), and energy prices have been front-page news ever since.

Ofgem raised the default tariff price cap by 54% from 1 April 2022. For a typical dual-fuel household paying by direct debit that is a jump from £1,277 to £1,971 a year ([Enerdata](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/ofgem-uk-will-raise-energy-price-caps-54-april-2022.html)). Those are national averages, not my bill, but the direction is clear enough.

Solar had been a "someday" item. With figures like that, someday became this spring.

## The quote

I went to Solar Services Scotland, and their quote came through on 4 April 2022. The kit list:

- 12 × Trina mono panels, 4 kW in total
- Van Der Valk mounting system
- Solis hybrid inverter (one box that handles both the panels and a battery)
- Generation meter
- Cabling and sundries
- 2 × Pylontech 3.55 kWh batteries
- Installation, £1,000
- £8,700 total, with 0% VAT

The quote also mentioned an EPC (Energy Performance Certificate) as a possible extra.

Then the batteries changed. The Pylontech units were out of stock, and the installer suggested 3 × Dyness B3 instead, for roughly £10k. Stock problems aren't the installer's fault, and offering an alternative is reasonable.

Still, I felt a bit under pressure to accept. Pylontech is the name that comes up again and again when you read about home batteries. Dyness was a brand I had barely heard of. Swapping the more credible name for an unfamiliar one, partway through, with a higher price attached, isn't the position I'd have chosen. I went with the Dyness, and I'll be keeping an eye on how they get on.

## The install

The panels went up in late April, in a single day. Honestly, nothing notable happened, which is about all you can ask of people working on your roof.

![Twelve panels in two rows on the south-west roof, seen from the drive](/assets/solar-panels-roof.jpeg)

All twelve panels are on the south-west facing roof, at roughly 220°. The inverter lives in the garage. The batteries are on back-order, so for now the system is running panels-only: whatever the roof generates is used in the house or exported to the grid.

![Drone shot looking down the lower row of panels along the roof edge](/assets/solar-panels-drone.jpeg)
